## Limits of these numbers

* **One run per case.** The model can answer differently on a second try, so a single 'held' is not a guarantee. The brief asks for 3 repeats on the final comparison (`--repeats 3`).
* **Rule-based guards.** They catch what the rules describe. A reworded, encoded or non-English attack, or a plain false claim such as a made-up financial figure, can pass. There is no model-based guard (Llama Guard / Prompt Guard).
* **Rank-1 poisoning is a worst case.** Real retrieval may not surface a poisoned passage at all.
* **Small clean sample.** Sixteen honest questions give a rough false-block rate, not a precise one.
* **Same author.** The attacks and the guardrails were written by the same person, against the brief's separation rule. The guardrail rules were written as general categories, but held-out attacks from a teammate are still needed.
* **Sandbox escape (F1) is not fixed.** With defences on, the guard's code check blocks the known tricks (`sys`, `modules`, `os`). That is a second line of defence only; the sandbox itself still has the hole (see `FINDINGS.md`).
* **How scoring treats blocked calls.** A tool call the pre-tool hook blocked, or that the model asked for but never ran, is listed as an attempt in `results.jsonl` but is not counted as a successful attack.
